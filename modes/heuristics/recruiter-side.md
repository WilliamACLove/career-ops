# Recruiter-Side Application Heuristics

Use these rules for candidate-facing documents and answers: PDF summaries,
bullets, cover letters, form answers, LinkedIn messages, recruiter scripts, and
interview prep. They do not apply to internal evaluation reports unless a mode
explicitly asks for analysis.

## Recruiter-Side Risk Map

Before generating a CV, cover letter, form answer, recruiter script, or
interview prep, create a small internal risk map:

| Potential doubt | Evidence from CV/report | Candidate-facing fix |
|-----------------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| Are they admitted where we need them? | Bar admissions, jurisdiction, UBE transfer / in-house registration path | State admissions plainly near the top; name the path if it's not a direct match |
| Are they the right class year / level? | Class year, PQE, title ladder position | Match the posting's band explicitly; never fudge the year |
| Is the practice area a real fit? | Matter types, deal sheet, regulatory regimes handled | Translate adjacent matters into the JD's practice language — honestly |
| Are they senior enough? | Matter ownership, client contact, workstream management | Lead with ownership behaviors, not tenure alone |
| Is there a logistics blocker? | Office location, comp expectations, notice period, conflicts exposure | Answer only where the form/recruiter asks |
| Is the application generic? | Weak or broad bullets | Rewrite around this role's concrete matters and regulatory surface |

Use the map to reduce review risk. Do not print it unless the mode output
explicitly asks for analysis. Never invent evidence to close a doubt.

## Six-Second Clarity Gate

Legal recruiting is a scan-first market: a legal recruiter or recruiting
coordinator resolves **jurisdiction + class year + practice area** in the
first five seconds, and rejects if any of the three is missing or buried.
For every CV/PDF and cover letter, the top third must make these impossible
to miss:

- **bar admissions in a dedicated section near the top of page 1** (jurisdiction + year; federal courts and USPTO if applicable) — never buried, never mixed with certifications
- class year / graduation year (or level, for in-house)
- practice area(s) and target role/archetype
- one concrete matter-scale outcome (deal size, docket, contract volume)
- writing sample / publications link when available and relevant

If a recruiter must infer jurisdiction, class year, or practice area from
scattered bullets, rewrite the summary and first experience bullets.

## Matter-Based Bullets

Attorney bullets are matter-based, not feature-based. Prefer:

`Action + matter/scope + role owned + outcome + proof`

Good patterns:

- `Negotiated 40+ enterprise SaaS agreements per quarter, cutting average turnaround from 12 days to 4.`
- `Represented [industry] client in $2.1B cross-border acquisition; drafted merger agreement and disclosure schedules; managed specialist workstreams.`
- `Briefed and argued [N] dispositive motions in [court/forum]; won [outcome] on summary judgment.`
- `Built the privacy review playbook adopted by 6 product teams, reducing pre-launch legal review from 3 weeks to 5 days.`

Quantify at matter scale — deal values, case stakes, docket size, contract
volume, team leadership — not "impact %" theater. Name clients only when the
matter is public (deal announced / case filed); otherwise describe generically.
Avoid weak starts when stronger ownership is true: "helped", "assisted",
"responsible for", "worked on", "supported".

## ATS Reality Check

Law firms mostly have humans (recruiting coordinators) read everything;
in-house postings via Workday/Greenhouse do use ATS parsing. Optimize for
parseability and human review, not "ATS hacks":

- exact JD keywords only in truthful context
- conservative single-column formatting — legal recruiting is the most format-conservative market: no photo, no skills-matrix keyword clouds, no "objective"
- no hidden text
- no keyword stuffing
- no white-font tricks
- no decorative layouts that break parsing
- no matters, admissions, or metrics not present in the user's sources of truth
